## Anti-Patterns Verdict

- **LLM Assessment**: The visual layout is clean and does a good job routing audiences. However, the use of generic emojis in card headers and a high number of external link cards makes the page read slightly like a link directory rather than a premium, integrated SaaS product.
- **Deterministic Scan**: Found 2 warnings. The `overused-font` warning flagged the use of `Fraunces` on line 17, and the `single-font` warning was a minor regex parser false positive since both `Fraunces` and `DM Sans` are loaded.
- **Visual Overlays**: Not executed (static file review).

## Overall Impression

The landing page does a superb job of communicating its value proposition and emotional resonance. The division into parental support and teacher utility is well structured. The single biggest opportunity is to align the branding (fonts/colors) closer to the web application itself and reduce outbound click leakage to external platforms.

## What's Working

1. **Empathy-Driven Copy**: Phrases like "You don't need to speak English to help your child" successfully target parental anxieties without condescension.
2. **Clear Audience Routing**: The Audience Router cards below the hero solve the product directory feel by providing immediate, structured pathways.

## Priority Issues

- **[P1] Hero Bilingual Friction**
  - **Why it matters**: Korean parents seeking homework help are immediately hit with a purely English hero section, which can cause bounce before they scroll to see the Korean translations.
  - **Fix**: Add a clear bicultural subtitle or Korean tagline above the primary display heading.
  - **Suggested command**: `/impeccable clarify`

- **[P2] Visual Identity Mismatch**
  - **Why it matters**: Switching from the navy/Fraunces theme of the landing page to the pitch-black/Space Grotesk theme of the web app creates a jarring transition that impacts brand trust.
  - **Fix**: Standardize typography (using Space Grotesk/Nunito) or visual accents (notebook lines/handwritten marks) across both layouts.
  - **Suggested command**: `/impeccable typeset`

- **[P2] Outbound Link Leakage**
  - **Why it matters**: A grid of external links (Spotify, TPT, Etsy, YouTube) bleeds clicks away from the core conversion funnel of trying the web app.
  - **Fix**: Visually differentiate the Web App card (making it full-width or highly accented) and group resources under a separate "Ecosystem" header.
  - **Suggested command**: `/impeccable layout`

- **[P3] Lack of FAQ / Help Support**
  - **Why it matters**: Anxious parents who encounter sheet scanning issues have no immediate support references or troubleshooting FAQs.
  - **Fix**: Add a short 3-item FAQ section near the footer.
  - **Suggested command**: `/impeccable harden`

## Persona Red Flags

- **Min-ji (Korean Parent)**:
  - Non-native speaker, very busy, looking for quick answers on mobile.
  - _Red Flag_: Entirely English hero section makes her think the app is too complex for her English level, prompting an immediate bounce.
- **Jordan (First-Time Teacher)**:
  - Wants to save time planning.
  - _Red Flag_: Multiple card links to Etsy, TPT, and YouTube make it hard to tell where the primary tool is, creating choice confusion.
- **Casey (Distracted Mobile)**:
  - Accessing the site one-handed.
  - _Red Flag_: Visual clutter of social links in the footer has touch targets placed too closely together, making fat-finger clicks common.

## Minor Observations

- Emojis in the product cards feel slightly less premium than custom SVG line icons.
- Video autoplay playback fallback works well for mobile.

## Questions to Consider

- Does the landing page need to list every single social platform in the footer?
- How can we visually emphasize that the Web App is a zero-friction, free browser entry?
